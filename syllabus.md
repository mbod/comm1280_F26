# Course Description, Goals and Objectives

-   This course introduces students to the central concepts, methods,
    and applications of data science (including network science) to
    questions in Communication. We focus on building data literacy,
    namely, the ability to find, appropriately handle, analyze, and
    communicate insights from a broad spectrum of data. Students will be
    introduced to key tools used in data science, including Python, R,
    and SQL as well as the appropriate use of Generative AI,
    particularly LLMs for these tasks.

-   The course also examines the practical and ethical challenges of big
    data, ubiquitous data tracking, and the increasing use of
    algorithmic decision systems and artificial intelligence. Students
    will develop skills working with large and complex datasets relevant
    to communication behaviors in the digital world, gain familiarity
    with basic programming for data analysis, and critically evaluate
    the use of data in media and other forms of communication.

-   **NOTE** No prior programming or data analysis experience is required.


## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students should be able to: 

-   Explain core principles of data literacy and applied data science in
    communication contexts
-   Identify different types, domains, and scales of data and evaluate
    their strengths and limitations
-   Use basic programming tools (Python and R) to conduct descriptive
    analyses of tabular and other forms of data
-   Create and interpret data visualizations
-   Relate analytical findings to theoretical, practical, and ethical
    concerns in communication
-   Critically evaluate data‑driven and algorithmic claims, including
    those produced by artificial intelligence systems

## Assesment Details

-   **Participation** (10%) 
    -   Students are expected to attend class regularly, come prepared,
        and participate actively in discussions and in‑class activities
        across both lecture and section meetings.

-   **Weekly Quizzes** (15%) 
    -   Short quizzes will be administered most weeks to assess
        understanding of key concepts, readings, and analytical
        ideas. Quizzes emphasize comprehension and application rather
        than memorization.

-   **Section Discussion Leadership** (10%) 
    -   Once during the semester, each student will lead a short
        discussion or activity during section. Activities may include
        evaluating a visualization, critiquing a dataset, or interpreting
        analytical results in a communication context.

-   **Weekly Assignments** (15%)
    -

-   **Midterm Group Multimedia Project** (20%) 
    -   Students will work in small groups to produce a multimedia project
        explaining a key concept from the first half of the course for a
        non‑expert audience. Acceptable formats include videos, podcasts,
        infographics, or interactive digital media. Projects must explain:
        -   The concept or method
        -   Why it is useful or important
        -   A real‑world communication example it helps illuminate

-   **Final Data Project** (30%) 
    -   The final project requires students to conduct a structured
        evaluation of a data‑driven or algorithmic claim relevant to
        communication. Students will assess the quality, assumptions,
        limitations, and implications of the claim using course concepts
        and evidence. The project includes a draft and revision stage.
